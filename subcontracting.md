## Why Primes Want You

First, understand the dynamic: prime contractors need qualified certified VOSBs/SDVOSBs to meet their subcontracting plans for larger government contracts. Large primes on federal contracts are *legally required* to submit subcontracting plans with goals for SDVOSB participation. This means they're actively looking for you — you're not just asking for a favor, you're solving their compliance problem. That's your leverage.

---

## The Four Main Channels

### 1. SBA SUBNet — The Most Direct Database
SBA's SUBNet (subcontracting network) helps large federal prime contractors find small subcontractors. Small businesses can browse opportunities filtered by state or keyword. This is the most direct, government-maintained listing of active subcontracting solicitations. Start here: **sba.gov/federal-contracting/contracting-guide/prime-subcontracting/subcontracting-opportunities**

### 2. The VA Prime Contractor Directory
The VA Prime Contractor Directory identifies large prime contractors that are required to establish subcontracting plans with goals. It includes company names, prime contract numbers, contract periods, NAICS codes, and points of contact — generated from Individual Subcontract Reports in the Electronic Subcontracting Reporting System. This is VA-specific and particularly valuable for your situation. Find it at **vetbiz.va.gov/resources/subcontracting**

### 3. USASpending.gov — Research Who Holds the Contracts
Before reaching out blindly, use **usaspending.gov** to research which companies hold the major VA IT contracts (particularly T4NG — Transformation Twenty-One Total Technology Next Generation). T4NG is the VA's enterprise contract covering IT and Health IT services including systems and software engineering, enterprise networks, cybersecurity, and operations and maintenance. Search for T4NG prime contractors, find their small business liaison contacts, and reach out directly. These firms have mandatory SDVOSB subcontracting goals they must hit.

### 4. Agency OSDBU Offices
Every major federal agency has an Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization (OSDBU). The DHS OSDBU, for example, lists large business prime contractors who are actively interested in subcontracting with SDVOSBs, including what capabilities they're looking for. The recommended approach is to research each prime's needs, register in their supplier database, and then contact their listed point of contact directly. The VA's OSDBU equivalent is their **Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization** — call them at 1-866-584-2344.

## The SBA Mentor-Protégé Program — The Most Powerful Option

This deserves special attention because it goes beyond simple subcontracting.

A mentor and its protégé can joint venture as a small business for any small business contract, provided the protégé individually qualifies as small. The joint venture may also pursue any type of set-aside contract for which the protégé qualifies, including SDVOSB set-asides.

What this means practically: you could partner with a large, established GovCon IT firm, form a joint venture, and bid on contracts together that you couldn't win alone — while the JV still qualifies for SDVOSB set-asides because *you* qualify.

Mentors can provide: guidance on business management, accounting, marketing, and strategic planning; financial assistance in the form of equity investments and loans; assistance navigating federal contract bidding and procurement; and business development help including identifying contracting and partnership opportunities.

**How to find a mentor:** The SBA does not match mentors and protégés — you must find your own through networking. Look at companies you've worked with previously, attend procurement conferences and industry days, and contact supplier diversity offices of large primes in your industry. Your local SBA district office and PTAC (Procurement Technical Assistance Center) can help with introductions.

## Networking Events — Where Relationships Actually Form

These are the venues where subcontracting relationships get built in person:

- **National Veterans Small Business Engagement (NVSBE)** — the VA's annual event specifically connecting veteran-owned firms with VA prime contractors and program managers. This is the single best event for your situation.
- **VetFedMatch** — NVSBC's VetFedMatch program creates conditions for federal prime contractors and SDVOSBs to learn about each other's subcontracting needs and business capabilities with the intention to create partnerships.
- **Procurement Technical Assistance Centers (PTACs)** — Texas has PTACs statewide (run through the Texas Veterans Commission and local universities) that provide free one-on-one matchmaking and bid assistance.
- **Industry Days** — when agencies issue a major solicitation, they often hold a public industry day. Attend these. Primes scope the room for subcontractors.
- **Boots to Business** — SBA's program specifically for transitioning veterans, available through military installations.

## The Practical Sequence for Your Team

Given where you are right now, here's the realistic order of operations:

1. **Get SAM.gov registered** — required before anything else. Takes 7–10 days to activate.
2. **Apply for SBA VetCert** (SDVOSB) — now processing in ~12 days. Do this immediately after formation.
3. **Identify your primary NAICS codes** — for web/software development these are typically 541511 (Custom Computer Programming), 541512 (Computer Systems Design), and 541519 (Other Computer Related Services). Your codes determine which contracts and set-asides you're eligible for.
4. **Research T4NG prime holders on USASpending.gov** — identify 10–15 firms with active VA IT contracts and find their small business liaisons.
5. **Register in prime contractor supplier portals** — most large GovCon firms (Leidos, SAIC, Booz Allen, Peraton, etc.) have online supplier registration.
6. **Attend NVSBE** — get on the VA's radar directly, and meet prime contractor business development staff in the same room.
7. **Contact your local PTAC** — free, and they often have existing relationships with primes looking for subcontractors in Texas.

The relationship-building side of this is where Lou's marketing skills become directly valuable — capability statements, one-pagers, and following up consistently with prime contractor contacts is exactly the work that wins subcontracts.
